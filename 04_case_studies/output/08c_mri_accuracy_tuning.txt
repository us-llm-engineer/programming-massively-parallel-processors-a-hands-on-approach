==================== MRI F^H d: ACCURACY AND TUNING (NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 Ti) ====================

---- A. Sine/cosine accuracy vs the size of the argument (1,000,000 random angles per range, double-precision reference) ----
[book] hardware trigonometry trades a little accuracy for a lot of speed. Max abs error of sin/cos:
|angle| up to    | raw __sinf     reduced __sinf   accurate sinf 
3.1              | 4.02e-07       4.93e-07         7.83e-08      
6.3              | 8.56e-07       6.81e-07         8.63e-08      
100.0            | 1.00e-05       7.19e-06         8.58e-08      
1000.0           | 1.36e-04       8.79e-05         8.53e-08      
10000.0          | 1.17e-03       7.84e-04         8.55e-08      
  Read: raw hardware sine/cosine degrades as |angle| grows; reducing the argument to one cycle first is somewhat better, but from about
  1000 rad up both are limited by the float ANGLE itself (spacing ~1e-4 rad there), which the accurate library functions also see
  (their error here is measured against the double sine of the same float angle, so it stays at float precision).

---- B. F^H d image agreement with the double-precision image (book: PSNR 27.6 dB vs 27.5 dB with fast trig) ----
4096 voxels x 8192 samples (1186.3 ms in double on the CPU). PSNR = 20 log10(max|I0| / sqrt(MSE)) on the magnitude image; 'rel RMS' = sqrt(MSE) / RMS(|I0|).
trigonometry                                   MSE          PSNR (dB)    rel RMS (%) 
accurate sinf/cosf (v4)                        1.748e-08    126.6        0.0001      
hardware, reduced to one cycle (v5)            1.616e-08    126.9        0.0001      
hardware, NO range reduction                   2.060e-08    125.9        0.0002      
  Read: the PSNR here measures agreement of the SAME F^H d image computed two ways, so it is far above the book's 27 dB, which
  measured a full reconstruction against a phantom (dominated by the reconstruction itself). What carries over is the ordering:
  accurate > reduced hardware >> raw hardware, and that a reduced hardware kernel costs almost nothing in image agreement.
