---- 1 + 2. Warp shape: how often do the 32 lanes of a warp disagree, and what does it cost? ----
A warp = 32 consecutive threads (x fastest). Its footprint in lattice points (and Angstrom, spacing 0.5) depends on the tile shape.
tile        warp footprint   warp extent (A)     | all in  all out MIXED   | lane use    | branchy   branchless  no test   | branchy/
x*y*z       (lattice pts)    x  y  z             | %       %       %       | in branch % | ms        ms          ms        | branchless
32x8x1      32x1x1           15.5 0.0 0.0        | 0.0     85.7    14.3    | 44.6        | 18.16     19.64       19.14     | 0.92     PASS
16x16x1     16x2x1           7.5 0.5 0.0         | 2.4     88.8    8.8     | 58.7        | 17.61     19.04       18.56     | 0.92     PASS
8x8x4       8x4x1            3.5 1.5 0.0         | 5.2     87.3    7.5     | 68.3        | 13.42     14.43       14.06     | 0.93     PASS
4x4x16      4x4x2            1.5 1.5 0.5         | 5.7     89.4    4.9     | 75.3        | 14.61     15.69       15.30     | 0.93     PASS
2x4x32      2x4x4            0.5 1.5 1.5         | 5.0     90.8    4.2     | 75.3        | 16.90     14.01       14.03     | 1.21     PASS
  MIXED = share of warp-level distance tests where some lanes are inside the sphere and others outside (divergent).
  lane use = of the lanes in the warp, the average fraction that are inside when the inside branch executes (1 lane of 32 = 3 %).
  'branchy' skips the rsqrt when outside; 'branchless' always computes it and multiplies by 0 or 1; 'no test' is the arithmetic
  lower bound (different physics, timing only). branchy/branchless < 1 means the divergent version is still faster.
  Both physical kernels are checked against a double-precision reference at 200 lattice points.
